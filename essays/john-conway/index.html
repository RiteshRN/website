A Tribute to John Conway

IN MEMORIAM · 1937–2020

John Conway was a research mentor of mine in high school — always kind and playful. He was like the Richard Feynman of mathematics.

John invented the game of life. He showed us that you can find stunning order amidst a sea of chaos. That simple rules can lead to complex behavior.

click to reload

John loved talking about his successes, but he loved his stupid games even more. He loved showing off his ability to tell you what day of the week a specific day was. January 24, 1984? It was a Tuesday. He could figure that out in three seconds.

He taught me not to get into the same fields that everyone else was interested in. He had seen a lot of smart young mathematicians get into Iwasawa theory after Andrew Wiles proved Fermat’s Last Theorem, and how all the low-hanging fruit was picked off by the time they learned the basics. Saved me a ton of wasted time.

I’m not sure I ever repaid my debt to him. I did one day teach him how to pronounce “Ramanujan” though — perhaps this will remain my greatest contribution to math.

I knew John in his 70’s, and even when he was in a wheelchair, he’d happily reach towards the chalkboard to share a new concept with us. He was so full of life.

RIP John. You played a great game buddy.
